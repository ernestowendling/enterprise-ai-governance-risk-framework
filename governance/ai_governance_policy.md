# AI Governance Policy

## Purpose and scope
This policy establishes accountable, risk-based governance for every AI system developed, purchased or used by the institution. It applies from idea to retirement, including models, generative AI, embedded vendor capabilities and material changes.

## Principles
AI must have a legitimate purpose, named business and risk owners, proportionate controls, reliable data, meaningful human accountability, traceable decisions and an approved lifecycle status. Customer interests, safety, privacy, security and regulatory duties take precedence over delivery speed.

## Requirements
The Business Owner must register the use before procurement or development. AI Governance assigns and challenges classification. High and critical uses require independent validation and committee approval. No system may operate beyond its approved use, risk acceptance or review date. Material incidents, changes or threshold breaches trigger reassessment.

## Accountability and assurance
The first line owns design, operation and evidence; the second line sets standards and challenges risk; Internal Audit provides independent third-line assurance. Exceptions require a risk owner, compensating controls, expiry and approval at least equivalent to the unmet control. Evidence is retained according to institutional record policy. The policy is reviewed annually by the AI Governance Committee.
